QI Resolved handles two kinds of data, and our legal role is different for each. The distinction matters, because it determines who you should contact about your data.

| | Data about you as a user | Data you record about buildings and clients |

| **Examples** | Your name, email, practice, billing details, how you use the app | Church records, client contacts, defect descriptions, photographs, plans, appendices |

## 4. Data we process for inspecting practices

When you use QI Resolved to record an inspection, the inspecting practice is the controller for what you enter. We hold it as a processor, under a written contract that meets Article 28 of the UK GDPR.

### 4.1 What inspection records contain

- **Building and site records** — church name, address, listing grade, denomination, diocese or parish, inspection date, weather conditions
- **Client contacts** — names, roles, email addresses and telephone numbers for churchwardens, PCC or fabric officers, incumbents and other correspondents
- **Plans** — uploaded floor plans and site plans, and the pin coordinates placed on them
- **Defect records** — descriptions, location references, defect identifiers, urgency ratings and estimated cost ranges
- **Photographs** — images taken on site, together with captions and any metadata the camera records, which can include time, device and location coordinates
- **Historic records** — defect data carried forward from previous quinquennial cycles
- **Appendices and supporting documents** — PAT testing and lightning conductor certificates, risk assessments, asbestos registers and reports, burial ground surveys, log books
- **Report drafts and generated PDFs**

### 4.2 Sensitive material that can appear in inspection records

Inspection material can contain more than building fabric, and practices should be aware of it:

- **Photographs may capture people** — congregation members, staff, contractors or visitors who happen to be present. An identifiable person photographed inside a place of worship may amount to data revealing religious belief, which is special category data under Article 9.
- **Named roles within a religious organisation** can themselves reveal religious belief. A churchwarden's name and email held against a named parish is an example.
- **Burial ground surveys** may identify living people, such as grave owners, faculty applicants or next of kin, even though data protection law does not apply to the deceased.
- **Risk assessments, accident records and asbestos documents** may name individuals and may include health information, which is also special category data.
- **Security-sensitive detail** — alarm and key-holder arrangements, lead roof coverings, the location of valuables — is not personal data but is treated as confidential.

We do not ask for special category data, and it is not a designed feature of the app. Where it arises incidentally, the inspecting practice is responsible for identifying its Article 9 condition. Practices should record only what the inspection requires, and should avoid photographing people where it can reasonably be avoided.

### 4.3 What we do as processor

We will:

- process inspection data only on the documented instructions of the practice;
- keep it confidential, and bind our staff to confidentiality;
- apply the security measures in section 8;
- engage sub-processors only with the practice's authorisation, and pass our own obligations down to them;
- help the practice respond to requests from individuals, and with data protection impact assessments and breach notification;
- notify the practice without undue delay if we become aware of a personal data breach; and
- delete or return inspection data at the end of the contract, except where we must keep it by law.

### 4.4 If you are a church, parish or client contact

If your details or photographs appear in an inspection because a practice inspected your building, that practice is the controller. Please contact them first. If you contact us, we will pass your request to the relevant practice and help them answer it, but we cannot act on their data without their instruction.

## 5. Anonymised and aggregated data

This section explains a commercial use we expressly reserve, and we would rather state it plainly than bury it.

We may create anonymised and aggregated datasets from data held in the app, including inspection records, and **we reserve the right to use those datasets for any lawful purpose, including commercially**.

### 5.1 What anonymisation means here

Before data enters an anonymised dataset we remove the information that identifies a person, a practice or a specific building. That includes church names and addresses, grid references and location coordinates, plan images, client and inspector contact details, practice identity, and metadata embedded in photographs. Free-text fields are stripped or reviewed, and figures are aggregated so that no individual, practice or building can be picked out of them.

We apply the Information Commissioner's guidance on anonymisation, including the motivated intruder test, and we treat the process as irreversible: we do not keep a key that would let us re-identify the data.

### 5.2 What we use it for

- Statistics on defect prevalence by building type, age, material and denomination
- Rates of deterioration between inspection cycles
- Repair cost benchmarks and the observed performance of repair types
- Product development, testing and quality assurance
- Training and evaluating machine learning models, including models that recognise defects in photographs
- Research, sector reporting and published findings
- Commercial products and services, including benchmark and market reports supplied to third parties

Photographs used to train models have their metadata removed and are filtered to exclude images containing identifiable people. An image of building fabric alone is not personal data.

### 5.3 What this means for your rights

Once data has been irreversibly anonymised it is no longer personal data under the UK GDPR. This policy and the rights in section 10 do not apply to it, we may keep and use it indefinitely, and it is not deleted when an account closes. Anonymisation is itself a form of processing, and we carry it out on the bases set out in sections 3 and 4.

### 5.4 The limit on inspection data

Inspection data is held on behalf of an inspecting practice, and the practice is its controller. **We anonymise and reuse that data only where our contract with the practice permits it.** The terms will set this out, and a practice will be able to ask what is included or object to it. Nothing in this policy overrides that contract.

### 5.5 What we do not do

We do not sell personal data. We do not share identifiable inspection records, client contact details or photographs of identifiable people with third parties for their own commercial purposes.

## 6. Who we share data with

We share personal data only with:

- **Service providers acting for us** — hosting, error reporting, email delivery, payment processing, customer support and professional advisers. Each is bound by contract to act only on our instructions, except where they are independent controllers in their own right, such as our payment provider.
- **Inspecting practices** — where we hold data as their processor.
- **Authorities and advisers** — where we are required to disclose by law, or need to establish or defend legal claims.
- **A buyer** — if the business or its assets are sold or reorganised, under confidentiality terms and subject to this policy.

We will publish a current list of sub-processors before launch and notify practices before adding a new one.

Personal data in the app is stored and processed **in the United Kingdom**. We do not routinely transfer it outside the UK.

If that changes, we will only transfer personal data to a country covered by UK adequacy regulations, or under appropriate safeguards such as the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, and we will update this policy and tell practices in advance.

## 8. Security

We protect data with measures appropriate to the risk, including:

- encryption in transit and at rest;
- access control on a least-privilege basis, with multi-factor authentication for administrative access;
- separation of each practice's data from every other practice's;
- logging and monitoring of administrative access;
- backups, tested restoration and a documented incident response procedure;
- confidentiality obligations and data protection training for staff; and
- security review of changes before release.

No system is completely secure. If a personal data breach is likely to result in a risk to people's rights, we will report it to the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and tell affected individuals where the risk is high. Where we are a processor, we will notify the practice without undue delay and support their reporting.

## 11. This website

This website is a holding page. It has no accounts, no forms and no login.

- **We set no cookies** and use no analytics or tracking.
